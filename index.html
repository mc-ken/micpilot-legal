What it does

Speak into your phone and MicPilot performs the action in your computer's browser:

  • Search — "search for the weather in Shanghai", or target a site: "search on YouTube for lofi beats"
  • Open — "open GitHub", "go to example.com"
  • Read the page aloud — the article you are looking at is spoken back to your phone
  • Summarise — "summarise this page" gives you the gist in a few sentences
  • Scroll and navigate — "scroll down", "back", "refresh", "go to the top"
  • Click anything by name — "click Sign in", "click Add to cart"
  • Type — "type hello world, then enter"
  • Tabs — "next tab", "close this tab", "what tabs do I have open?"
  • Video and audio — "pause", "skip forward 30 seconds", "mute", "volume up"
  • Ask DeepSeek — "what is quantum entanglement?", and the answer is read back to you

Private by design

No servers of ours

We do not operate any backend that receives your data. Your phone talks directly to a small helper program running on your own computer, over your own local network.

Bring your own key

Around 60 everyday commands work entirely offline, with no key and no internet. The DeepSeek API key is optional and is stored only on your machine.

Nothing collected

No accounts, no analytics, no cookies, no browsing history. Nothing to hand over, because nothing ever reaches us.

How it works

  1. Run the included helper on your computer — one command, Node.js, no dependencies.
  2. Load the Chrome extension using the step-by-step guide.
  3. Open the controller page on your phone, enter the pairing code shown in your browser, click Allow on your computer, and start talking.

Requirements

  • Chrome 116 or newer on Windows, macOS, or Linux
  • Node.js 18 or newer on that same computer
  • An Android or iOS phone on the same Wi-Fi network
  • Optional: your own DeepSeek API key for the AI features

What it is not

MicPilot controls your own browser, on your own computer, on your own local network. It cannot reach another person's device: the helper only accepts connections from the machine it runs on, and pairing requires a code displayed inside your own browser.
