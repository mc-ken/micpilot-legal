What it does

Speak into your phone and MicPilot performs the action in your computer's browser:

  • Search — "search for the weather in Shanghai", or target a site: "search on YouTube for lofi beats"
  • Open — "open GitHub", "go to example.com"
  • Read the page aloud — the article you are looking at is spoken back to your phone
  • Summarise — "summarise this page" gives you the gist in a few sentences
  • Scroll and navigate — "scroll down", "back", "refresh", "go to the top"
  • Click anything by name — "click Sign in", "click Add to cart"
  • Type — "type hello world, then enter"
  • Tabs — "next tab", "close this tab", "what tabs do I have open?"
  • Video and audio — "pause", "skip forward 30 seconds", "mute", "volume up"
  • Ask DeepSeek — "what is quantum entanglement?", and the answer is read back to you

Private by design

Encrypted end to end

Your commands are encrypted between your phone and your browser. The relay that connects them only ever forwards ciphertext — it cannot read what you said, and it cannot see the pages you asked for. Rather keep everything inside your own network? You can run the relay yourself instead.

Bring your own key

Around 60 everyday commands work entirely offline, with no key and no internet. The DeepSeek API key is optional and is stored only on your machine.

Nothing collected

No accounts, no analytics, no cookies, no browsing history. We never receive your commands or the pages you read in a readable form, so there is nothing to hand over.

How it works

  1. Install the Chrome extension — that is the only thing you install.
  2. Open the link the extension gives you on your phone. It pairs by itself — nothing to type.
  3. Click Allow on your computer once, then start talking. Your phone does not need to be on the same Wi-Fi.

Requirements

  • Chrome 116 or newer on Windows, macOS, or Linux
  • An Android or iOS phone — any network, 4G or Wi-Fi
  • Optional: your own DeepSeek API key for the AI features

What it is not

MicPilot controls your own browser, on your own computer. It cannot reach another person's device: pairing needs a code that is only shown inside your own browser, and you have to click Allow on that computer to let a phone in.
