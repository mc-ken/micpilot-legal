Privacy Policy

Effective date: 29 September 2026  ·  Last updated: 29 September 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how MicPilot (Guang Li, an individual developer — "we", "us") handles information in connection with the MicPilot browser extension, the local helper program, and the mobile controller web page (together, the "Software").

1. The short version

  • We do not collect, store, or have access to any of your personal data.
  • The Software has no accounts, no sign-up, and no analytics.
  • Your commands are end-to-end encrypted between your phone and your browser. Whether you connect through the helper on your own computer or through a relay, the messages are encrypted in a way the relay cannot read. The relay forwards ciphertext — it cannot see what you said, and it cannot see the pages you asked it to read.
  • If you use the helper on your own computer, nothing leaves your local network at all.
  • Your AI provider's API key is stored in your own browser and is never sent to us — not even encrypted.
  • The only external service the Software can contact is the API of your chosen AI provider (DeepSeek), and only when you have enabled that feature with your own API key.

2. Who we are

The Software is published by Guang Li, trading as MicPilot, contactable at szmcken168@gmail.com. For the purposes of the EU/UK General Data Protection Regulation ("GDPR"), we are not a data controller or processor for the content you process with the Software: in local mode it never reaches us, and in relay mode it reaches us only as ciphertext for which we hold no key. The only personal data a relay of ours could technically handle is connection metadata — IP addresses and connection times — held in memory only and discarded when the connection ends (see section 3c). We never write it to disk and never build any profile from it.

3. What the Software does with information

a) Voice input

When you speak into the mobile controller page, speech recognition is performed by your browser and your device's speech recognition provider — for example Google's speech services in Chrome, or Apple's on iOS. The audio is handled by that provider under their own privacy policy. We never receive the audio, and we never receive the resulting transcript.

b) Commands and page content

Your recognised text is sent from your phone to the browser extension, which performs the action. If you ask the Software to read or summarise a page, the text of that page is read locally by the browser extension. All of this is encrypted end-to-end between your phone and your browser (see section 3c), so nothing in between can read it.

How the two devices reach each other depends on which mode you chose:

  • Local helper (default when you run it): your phone talks directly to the helper program on your own computer, over your own local network. Nothing leaves your network.
  • Relay mode (optional): both your phone and your browser connect out to a relay, which forwards the encrypted messages between them. See section 3c for exactly what a relay can and cannot see, and how to host one yourself.

c) The relay server

If you choose the relay mode — rather than running the helper on your own computer — your phone and your browser each open an encrypted connection to a relay, which forwards messages between them. This mode exists so that people who would rather not keep a program running on their computer can still use the Software. The relay can be one that we host, or one you run yourself: the Software includes everything needed to run your own.

What the relay can see:

  • the room code that pairs your two devices;
  • the technical facts any server sees: IP addresses, connection times, and message sizes;
  • the public keys your two devices exchange so they can agree on a shared secret.

What the relay cannot see:

  • your voice commands or any text you type — these are encrypted before they leave your device;
  • the content of any page you ask it to read or summarise;
  • your AI provider's API key, which never leaves your browser.

Rooms are held in memory only. We do not write messages to disk, we do not keep a transcript, and restarting the relay erases everything. Access logs on the relay are discarded.

If you prefer that nothing pass through anyone else's infrastructure at all, use the local helper instead — the Software behaves identically, and the two modes are interchangeable.

d) AI features (optional)

If — and only if — you enter your own DeepSeek API key, certain text is sent directly from your computer to DeepSeek's API at https://api.deepseek.com for processing:

  • the voice command itself, so it can be interpreted as an action; and
  • page text, when you explicitly ask for a summary or a spoken answer about the current page.

That traffic goes directly from your machine to DeepSeek. It does not pass through us. DeepSeek's handling of that data is governed by DeepSeek's own terms and privacy policy. You can disable all AI features at any time, in which case no data leaves your computer and only the built-in offline command engine is used.

e) Your API key

Your DeepSeek API key is stored in plain text in a configuration file inside the folder where the helper program runs, on your own computer. It is transmitted only to DeepSeek, as a request header. We never receive it, store it, or have any way to read it.

f) Pairing

Pairing between your phone and your computer uses a short code displayed inside your own browser. After pairing, your phone stores a random access token in its own browser storage. All of this happens on your own devices.

4. What we do NOT do

We do not:

  • collect, transmit, or store your browsing history, page contents, keystrokes, or voice recordings;
  • operate any backend service, database, or log store that can read the data you process with the Software. In relay mode a relay forwards messages it cannot decrypt (section 3c), and in local mode no server of ours is involved at all;
  • use cookies, tracking pixels, fingerprinting, or any third-party analytics;
  • sell, rent, or share any information with advertisers or data brokers;
  • create user profiles of any kind.

5. Permissions the extension requests, and why

Permission Why it is needed
Read and change data on websites you visit Required so that commands such as "read this page", "click Sign in", "find on page" and "scroll down" can act on the page you are currently viewing. Page content is processed locally, and is only sent anywhere if you explicitly ask for an AI summary with your own key.
Tabs Required to open, close, switch, reload and navigate tabs on your command.
Storage Required to remember your settings and pairing code locally in your browser.
Alarms Required to keep the connection to your local helper program alive.

The Software does not use remote code. All executable code is contained in the extension package.

6. Local network security

The mobile controller page is served by the helper program on your computer. By default this is plain HTTP over your local network, which means other devices on the same network could, in principle, observe that traffic. Pairing requires a code displayed in your browser, and access tokens are random, but if you are on an untrusted network you should run the helper in its HTTPS mode. This is documented in the setup guide.

7. Data retention and your rights

Because we never receive your data, there is nothing for us to retain, export, correct, or delete. Everything the Software stores lives on your own devices:

  • the helper's configuration file (including your AI key, if provided) in the helper's folder;
  • your settings and pairing token in your browser's local storage.

You can delete all of it at any time by removing those files and clearing the site's data in your browser. If you are in the EU/UK and believe we hold data about you, contact us at szmcken168@gmail.com and we will respond within 30 days.

8. Children

The Software is not directed at children under 16, and we do not knowingly process data from children.

9. Third-party services

The Software can interact with the following third parties, each under their own terms:

  • DeepSeek (api.deepseek.com) — only when you enable AI features with your own key.
  • Your browser's speech recognition provider (Google, Apple, or your device vendor) — only when you use voice input.
  • Gumroad or the Chrome Web Store — the store through which you purchased the Software. Your purchase is governed by that store's privacy policy; we receive only the email address and order details that the store passes to sellers so we can provide support and refunds.
  • The host of our website — this page is served by GitHub Pages. As with any web host, GitHub records the IP addresses of visitors in its server logs. We have no access to those logs.
